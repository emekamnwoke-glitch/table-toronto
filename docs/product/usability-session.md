# Usability session: the nearby list

**Status:** Plan, not run. For a small, consented session after the privacy
fixes in the event contract are in place.

## What this is, and is not

A handful of one-to-one sessions to learn whether the nearby list helps
people choose and continue. It produces **early usability observations**.
It does not produce conversion, demand-lift or booking evidence: the
availability is simulated, the sample is tiny, and participants know they
are being watched. Anything written up must say so.

## Question

When someone asks for a table near them, does the ranked list (nearest
first, with its reason shown) help them pick an option and decide whether to
continue? Where do they hesitate, misread, or ignore it?

## Before anyone takes part

- **Consent, in writing, before starting.** Say what is recorded (screen
  and talk-aloud if you record; the journey events described in the event
  contract), that the app is a demonstration with simulated availability and
  makes no real booking, that they can stop at any time, and how to ask for
  their data to be deleted. Do not record anyone who has not agreed.
- **No real location needed.** Have participants pick a spot on the map
  (their street is fine, a nearby landmark is better). The app does not
  store coordinates or exact distances, but a screen recording would show
  the map, so offer a landmark instead.
- **Real accounts are not required.** Create a throwaway account per
  participant. Delete accounts and their events afterwards if they ask, and
  at the end of the study regardless.
- **Run the privacy checks first:** per-restaurant distances are not stored
  (only rank and a coarse band), the pseudonym key is set, and rate limits
  are on.

## Tasks (about 15 minutes)

1. "Imagine you want to eat out tonight with one other person. Use this to
   decide where." Observe: location choice, how they read the list, which
   option they open and why.
2. "Open the one you'd consider and tell me what you'd do next." Observe:
   whether they understand the times are simulated, and whether they
   continue.
3. "What would make you trust this list more or less?" Ask last.

## What to note

- Whether they notice and understand "320 m away (straight line)" and the
  note that availability is simulated and does not affect the order.
- Whether they expected more than proximity (cuisine, price, reviews) and
  said so unprompted.
- Where they got stuck: the map pick, the time limits (17:00-21:30), the
  demo hand-off screen.
- Whether anyone believed the booking was real. If so, the labelling has
  failed and must be fixed before anything else.

## How to report it

Quote what people said and did, count them as "3 of 5 participants", and
state the number of participants every time. Do not report rates, lift or
"conversion". The funnel report from these sessions is simulated and covers
a handful of journeys; it checks that the instrumentation works, nothing
more. Findings feed the next decision: which second signal to add and
whether the reason line is enough.
